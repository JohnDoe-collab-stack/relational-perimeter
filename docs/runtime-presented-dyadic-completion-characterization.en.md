# Runtime characterization of presented dyadic completion

## Status and scope

This passage is the canonical text for the
`RUNTIME_PRESENTED_DYADIC_COMPLETION_CHARACTERIZATION` claim. It describes the
produced completeness of the relational carrier and its relative
characterization among explicitly presented constructive dyadic completions.

The characterization concerns executable data: signed-dyadic approximations,
explicit moduli, positive coherence laws, and Cauchy families equipped with
their witnesses. It does not start from an extensive real-number type and does
not construct all classical real numbers. Behaviors, executions, and rich
productions remain constitutive; the numerical carrier and the compared
presentations are downstream readings.

## Formal presented-dyadic-completion conclusion

For every perimetral presentation `P`, every depth `baseDepth`, and every
explicitly received `CarrierFamily`, `carrierCompletion P baseDepth family`
constructs a `CarrierCompletionResult`. The result retains the whole
`NativeCompleteness` packet of the corresponding native family and only then
projects its produced limit to an `EffectiveCauchyReal.Carrier`. It supplies a
convergence law for the original carrier family and proves uniqueness of the
limit up to `EffectiveCauchyReal.Equivalent`.

Production receives no candidate limit. `carrierNativeFamily` forms the native
family from its terms, outer modulus, and the terms' own moduli. Its Cauchy law
consumes the current `Step`s owned by the executions and connects them to the
received normalized approximations. `nativeCompleteness` then executes native
production and grouping. The projection `toCarrier native.limit` occurs only
after this production, and the native packet remains available in the result.

`carrierCompletion_perimeterIndependent` compares two productions obtained
from the same carrier family over two presentations or depths. Its conclusion
is a positive numerical equivalence derived from uniqueness; it identifies
neither runtimes nor their archives, journals, or occurrences.

## Non-circular completion contract

`PresentedDyadicData` receives a carrier type, its approximations and moduli,
positive monotonicity and coherence laws, and an embedding of dyadic codes with
its exactness law. `PresentedDyadicStructure` adds a positive equivalence
connected in both directions to quantitative approximation equivalence.

A `PresentedFamily` contains its terms, monotone outer modulus, and Cauchy law.
`Converges` exposes a threshold and the required quantitative comparisons.
`PresentedDyadicCompletion.complete` receives only the family and must produce
a `LimitResult` containing the value, its convergence, and its uniqueness. No
candidate limit, terminal map, or inverse readout occurs in this contract.

The realization `carrierCompletionStructure P baseDepth` concretely closes the
contract for `carrierPresentedCore` by invoking the preceding native
production. The canonical addition, negation, and multiplication families
specify only the expected numerical behavior; they define no runtime producer
and do not choose an arbitrary received family.

## Ordered realization and finite witnesses

`PresentedDyadicOrderedFieldData` enriches a presented completion with zero,
one, addition, negation, multiplication, inversion under apartness, constructive
order, algebraic laws, order laws, dyadic density, and natural bounds.
Convergence of the operations to their canonical families belongs to the
contract. `PresentedDyadicOrderedFieldCompletion` joins this packet with the
completion without assuming a comparison morphism.

`carrierOrderedCompletion P baseDepth` concretely realizes this contract. The
carrier operations are numerical readouts already connected to constituted
runtime operations. Their laws are transported from relational producers and
their readout theorems; they are not imported back to manufacture those
producers.

Density and natural bounds are first constructed as `CarrierDensityResult` and
`CarrierNaturalBoundResult`. These packets retain the realized behaviors,
probes, actions, targets, and produced order witnesses. The ordered contract
projects their codes and bounds only after this production. Readout-independence
theorems consume the retained `Step`s and actions; they do not reconstruct an
independent comparison.

Quantitative transports explicitly pay their precision losses. Multiplicative
congruence retains the required crossed amplitudes, and order transport aligns
all seven certificates at a common precision. No modulus cofinality, global
order decision, or classical arithmetic principle is assumed.

## Relative characterization

A `CompletionMorphism Source Target` contains a map, preservation of
equivalence, agreement on dyadic constants, and a numerical presentation for
every image. It contains no inverse, round-trip law, or preservation of limits
as an assumption.

`comparisonMorphism Source Target` is produced by target completeness from the
source's numerical presentation. Its pointwise uniqueness is derived from
uniqueness of limits. `preservePresentedLimit` and `mapProducedLimit` derive
limit preservation through a strengthened diagonal family; preservation is not
a received field of the morphism.

For every `Target : PresentedDyadicOrderedFieldCompletion`,
`relativeOrderedCharacterization P baseDepth Target` constructs forward and
backward morphisms between the carrier completion and `Target`. It proves their
returns up to behavioral equivalence, preservation and reflection of
equivalence, order, and apartness, preservation of zero, one, addition,
negation, multiplication, and inversion, and transport of density, bounds, and
produced limits in both directions.

Density and bound transports retain the code or natural numbers of the
received witness; they do not execute the target's own selector. Round trips
are not equalities of functions, carriers, occurrences, or rich families. They
do not recover moduli, Cauchy witnesses, banks, archives, or journals forgotten
by readout.

## Non-conclusions

This characterization covers Cauchy families explicitly presented as
`CarrierFamily` and every constructive target satisfying the
`PresentedDyadicOrderedFieldCompletion` contract. It establishes a relative
characterization of presented dyadic completion, not an identification with a
particular external classical construction.

It proves neither coverage of all classical real numbers, least-upper-bound
completeness, nor globally decidable order. It constructs no executable map
from a bare extensive quotient. Comparison with the usual real numbers still
requires an exact external reference and a separate gate proving totality,
correctness, faithfulness, and structural transports in the selected scope.
No conclusion about `AC`, `CH`, physical cost, or originality follows from this
result.
